Travel Distance Calculator Guide

How the carbon footprint survey and its distance calculator work: what delegates see, where answers are stored, and how to set it up and test it.

Open the survey Open the calculator

1. What this is

The carbon footprint survey is a Cognito form. It is shown on a page next to a small distance calculator. A delegate types where each journey started and ended, and the calculator works out the km. They then type that number into the survey's "One-way distance" box. They no longer need to work out distances themselves.

The survey and its results stay in Cognito. The calculator only helps delegates find a number. It does not change the survey questions or where answers are stored.

2. What happens, step by step

Delegate opens the new survey link

They see the same survey as before. On a computer, the calculator is a card on the right. On a phone, it opens from a "Distance calculator" button at the bottom of the screen.

↓

They use the calculator for each journey

They pick the mode (Air, Rail, Vehicle or Ferry), type where they started and where they ended (an address, a place, a hotel or an airport) and pick the right match from the suggestions that appear as they type, then press Calculate. The calculator shows the km and which places it matched, so they can check them.

↓

They copy the number into the survey

They press Copy, or type the km, into the "One-way distance travelled with this mode (in km)" box for that journey. They repeat for each journey (Add Journey).

↓

They press Submit

Cognito receives the answers, exactly as it does today.

↓

The entry appears in Cognito

The survey administrator logs in to Cognito, opens the survey, and goes to Entries. Results can be viewed, searched and exported to Excel.

3. Where the information goes

InformationWhere it goesStored?
Survey answers (name, email, journeys, distances)The Cognito account that owns the survey (the VUKA1 account), in the survey's EntriesYes, by Cognito
Places typed into the calculatorSent as they are typed to Photon (to suggest places), and to OpenStreetMap (to find a place) and OSRM (to find the driving route), only to get a distanceNo. Our page does not save them
The calculator page itselfHosted free on GitHub PagesIt holds no delegate data
The calculator never passes a delegate's answers to us, and nothing from the survey reaches GitHub. Only Cognito holds the survey data.

4. Setup and testing (for the survey administrator)

Step 1: test it

  1. Open the survey link on a computer and on a phone.
  2. Try the calculator: Cape Town International Airport to Harare International Airport, Zimbabwe by air. It should give about 2,170 km.
  3. Submit one test entry with fake details (name "TEST", a small distance).
  4. In Cognito, open Entries and check it arrived with the right answers.
  5. Check whether a notification email arrives, if the form is set up to send one.
  6. Delete the test entry.

Step 2: go live

  1. Replace the old Cognito link with the new survey link in emails, social posts and messages.
  2. Optionally, add a line near the distance question: "Not sure of the distance? Use the calculator beside the form."
  3. Check Entries in Cognito while the survey runs.

The old link still works, but delegates using it will not see the calculator.

5. Limits to be aware of

WhatWhat it means for the data
Distances are estimatesFine for carbon footprint reporting, not for navigation.
Air and ferryStraight-line distance. Real flights are usually a little longer.
RailEstimated from the road distance plus 5%. It is approximate.
VehicleDriving route. It may avoid a border or ferry the person actually used.
Wrong place matchedIf a delegate types only "Paris", the tool may pick the wrong one. The calculator shows what it matched so they can fix it by adding the country.
Place not foundThe tool says so and gives no number. This is safe. Delegates can still type their own estimate.
Free servicesOpenStreetMap and OSRM are free and shared. They can be slow at busy times, with no guarantee of uptime.
Privacy tip for delegates: they do not need to enter their exact home address. Their suburb or nearest landmark is accurate enough.

6. If something goes wrong

ProblemWhat to do
A delegate says the calculator can't find their placeThe calculator explains why and suggests similar places to tap. Otherwise ask them to retype it with the city and country (for example "Hotel name, Harare, Zimbabwe"), or to type their own estimate in the survey.
The calculator shows an error or is slowWait a minute and try again. If it keeps failing, delegates can type their own estimate. Tell Hlulani.
Looks different or old after an updatePress Ctrl + F5 to refresh. Browsers keep an old copy for a few minutes.
Submit doesn't work or no entry appears in CognitoCheck the survey is open and its settings in Cognito. If the old Cognito link works but ours does not, tell Hlulani straight away.
A distance looks wrong in the resultsCheck the places the delegate typed. Unusually large or small values are worth reviewing before using the data.
